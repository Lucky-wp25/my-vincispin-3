How the peg pyramid works

The board is a triangle of offset pegs, with each row slightly wider than the one above it. A ball dropped at the top has an even chance of going left or right at each peg, then repeats that decision all the way down. After the last row, it settles into one of the bottom pockets, which are always one more than the number of rows.

Because every path is built on repeated fifty-fifty turns, balls gather near the middle and only a few travel far enough to land at the edges. Mathematicians describe the shape as a Galton board and the result as a binomial curve. The payout table simply mirrors that shape: common central pockets pay less, while rare edge pockets pay much more.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket value is set close to the inverse of its probability, then reduced a little to create the house margin. When the probability of every pocket is multiplied by its payout, the total comes out just under one, and that gap is the operator’s edge. Most published versions sit around a 97% to 99% return to player, depending on the studio and the chosen settings.

A sensible rule of thumb: no row count or risk setting can turn the game in your favour over time. The switches only change variance — how uneven the ride feels — not the long-term maths.

Licensed versions use a certified random number generator, and provably fair builds publish seed data so the result can be checked later. What they cannot do is let anyone steer the ball toward a chosen pocket.

Straight answers to five common questions

Is every drop truly random?

On licensed platforms, yes. The ball path is driven by an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you verify later that the result was fixed before the drop.

Do more rows improve the payout?

No. Extra rows add more pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge prizes grow while their chance falls. The long-run return stays broadly the same across board sizes.

Which risk level is best?

None is mathematically superior. Low risk keeps results smoother, while high risk concentrates value into rarer edge hits. The right setting depends on budget and temperament.

Does timing the button affect the result?

No. The landing pocket is determined by the random generator, not by how quickly you press the button. Any pattern you notice is usually coincidence.
